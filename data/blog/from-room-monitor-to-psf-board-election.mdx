In 2019, I walked into PyConf Hyderabad as a room monitor.

I wasn't thinking about leadership. I wasn't thinking about the Python Software Foundation Board. I certainly wasn't imagining that, a few years later, I would be one of the candidates in a PSF Board of Directors election.

I was simply looking for an opportunity to volunteer and contribute.

Seven years later, I found myself participating in the 2026 PSF Board election.

For me, that experience is something worth pausing and reflecting on. Not because of the election result, and not to make a case for my candidacy, but because of the journey that brought me here and what it has taught me about community, responsibility, and creating opportunities for others.

![Kalyan Prasad at a Python community event](/static/images/psf-board-election-journey.jpg)

## Starting with a small opportunity

My involvement with the Python community began with that small volunteering role at PyConf Hyderabad in 2019.

Over time, I was trusted with more responsibilities. I became involved in different parts of organizing conferences and communities, including programs, sponsorship, logistics, speakers, volunteers, operations, and Code of Conduct work.

Eventually, I had opportunities to serve as Co-Chair and later Chair of PyConf Hyderabad, as Co-Chair of PyCon India 2023, and as Lead Organizer of HydPy.

My community involvement also gradually extended beyond India.

Since 2022, I have had opportunities to contribute to program and review activities across conferences including PyCon US, EuroPython, PyCon JP, PyCon APAC, PyData Global, JupyterCon, and SciPy. I have also contributed through the PSF Diversity & Inclusion Working Group and the NumFOCUS Code of Conduct and Small Development Grants Working Groups.

None of this happened through one big decision.

It happened one opportunity, one responsibility, and one community interaction at a time.

## 2026 has been a special year

Earlier this year, I was honored to receive the Python Software Foundation Community Service Award.

I saw that recognition not as a destination, but as encouragement to continue contributing and to take on greater responsibility where my experience could be useful.

Then came the decision to put my name forward for the PSF Board of Directors.

Being part of the election has itself been a learning experience.

Writing my [nomination statement](https://www.python.org/nominations/elections/2026-python-software-foundation-board/nominees/kalyan-prasad/) made me think more deeply about what years of community involvement had actually taught me. The [PSF candidate interview](https://pyfound.blogspot.com/2026/08/kalyan-prasad-2026-psf-board-election.html) gave me another opportunity to articulate those experiences. I also opened an [**AMA**](https://discuss.python.org/t/psf-board-nomination-ama-kalyan-prasad/108743/) so community members could ask questions about my candidacy, including anything that wasn't covered in my nomination statement or candidate interview.

These experiences have reminded me that community leadership is not about having all the answers.

The Python community is global, diverse, and shaped by very different local realities. No single person's experience can represent all of it.

Sometimes, one of the most important things we can bring is a willingness to listen.

## Bringing local experiences into global conversations

There is another aspect of this year's experience that means a lot to me personally.

I am the only candidate from India in the 2026 PSF Board election and one of three candidates highlighted from the broader Asia-Pacific Python community.

I don't see that as representing an entire country or region. No individual can do that.

Instead, it reminds me why it is important for people from different local communities to participate in global conversations.

Many of the lessons I carry with me come from organizing communities in Hyderabad and India: working with volunteers, finding sponsors, building conference teams, creating inclusive spaces, dealing with limited resources, transferring knowledge, and trying to prevent communities from becoming dependent on the same few people year after year.

Those experiences may be local, but many of the challenges are shared by communities around the world.

Global communities become stronger when people can bring those different experiences into the same conversation, while remaining willing to listen to experiences very different from their own.

## The lesson I keep coming back to

One idea has become increasingly important to me over the years:

**Healthy communities create opportunities for new people to take ownership.**

I experienced that personally.

HydPy gave me an opportunity to start as a volunteer. Over time, the community trusted me with greater responsibilities. Today, one of the things that gives me the most satisfaction is seeing newer volunteers step forward, take ownership, and become the visible organizers of the community.

Community growth isn't only about increasing attendance or organizing more events.

It is also about creating a path where someone can arrive as an attendee, become a volunteer, contribute an idea, become a speaker or mentor, take responsibility, and perhaps eventually become a community leader themselves.

If the same people always have to run everything, we haven't really built a sustainable community.

## The result, and what I take from it

The [results of the 2026 PSF Board election](https://opavote.com/results/6289918744854528) are now out, and I was not elected to the Board this time.

This was my first time standing for the PSF Board, alongside 16 other candidates for four available seats. Seeing the support I received from members of the Python community meant a lot to me.

Of course, I would have been happy to have the opportunity to serve on the Board. But when I looked at the result, my first feeling was gratitude.

People took the time to read my nomination, learn about my journey, participate in the AMA, and place their trust in me through their votes. I don't take that support for granted.

Going from a room monitor at a Python conference in Hyderabad to participating in a global PSF Board election is something I could not have planned when I started volunteering in 2019.

And I didn't make that journey alone.

Communities gave me opportunities. Organizers trusted me. Volunteers worked alongside me. Mentors shared their experiences. People from Python communities in India and around the world welcomed me into conversations and gave me opportunities to contribute.

That is perhaps the biggest thing I take away from this experience.

You don't always know where a small contribution will lead.

Sometimes you simply show up, volunteer for something, learn from the people around you, and keep contributing.

Years later, you look back and realize just how far that first small opportunity allowed you to travel.

Being a candidate in the 2026 PSF Board of Directors election has been one of those moments for me.

I didn't win a seat this time, but I came away from the experience with something valuable: new perspectives, conversations with people across the community, and an even stronger motivation to keep contributing.

The journey continues. I am looking forward to continuing to learn, contribute, and, most importantly, help create similar opportunities for others.
